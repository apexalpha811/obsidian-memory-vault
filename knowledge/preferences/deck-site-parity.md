# Paired deliverables must mirror each other

**Rule:** When a deliverable ships as a pair (a PowerPoint deck and its HTML landing page,
a document and its web version), the two must always mirror each other. A change to one is
not finished until the same change is made to the other, in the same pass, unprompted.

Scope of "mirror": branding and logo treatment, structure (navigation, section order, which
sections exist), content (copy, pricing, disclaimers), and any bug fix or correction.

It also applies **across** sibling deliverables that ship as one package. If two of three
vertical packages have a nav bar and the third does not, that is a defect, not a stylistic
choice, even when nobody specified a nav bar.

**Why:** Stated by the user after I updated the HYGIA wordmark to logo art in all three
PowerPoint decks but left the three HTML pages on typed text, and separately noticed a
missing nav bar on one page and raised it as an open question instead of fixing it. Packages
that visibly disagree with each other look unfinished when sent to a client, and surfacing
parity gaps as questions pushes the reconciliation work back onto the user.

**How to apply:** Before reporting any vertical-package work complete, ask what the mirrored
change is in the sibling artifact and in the sibling verticals, and make it. Do not present
parity gaps as optional follow-ups. Fix, then report. Parallel subagent builds are the main
way this drift gets introduced, so when delegating per-vertical work, specify the shared
structural elements explicitly in every handoff packet rather than leaving them to each
agent's judgment.
